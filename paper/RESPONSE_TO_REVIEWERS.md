## 1. Active Software Contribution: Zero-Copy Dynamic Tool-RAG Schema Pruner ($54 \rightarrow \text{Top-}5$ Tools)
- **Reviewer Concern (Scientific Contribution 3/5 $\rightarrow$ 4/5):** Prior versions diagnosed enterprise schema prompt bloat (54 tools) without shipping an active client-side software mitigation inside the orchestrator workers.
- **Resolution (`benchmark/picoclaw_orch.go`, `benchmark/openclaw_orch.mjs`, `benchmark/python_orch.py`, `scripts/run_tool_rag_mitigation_experiment.py`):**
  - We implemented an active **Zero-Copy Dynamic Tool-RAG Schema Pruner** (`OPENCLAW_MODE="tool_rag"` / `picoclaw_tool_rag`, `openclaw_tool_rag`) across all three language workers. Before serializing the HTTP payload to `llama-server`, the orchestrator initializes the full 54-tool corporate catalog ($N_{\text{schemas}} = 54$) and computes an in-memory 384-dimensional cosine similarity dot-product against the workload query embedding to prune the catalog down to the $\text{Top-}5$ semantically relevant tools.
  - Physical benchmarking across W1–W4 in both Regime A (`cache_prompt=false`) and Regime B (`cache_prompt=true`) ($n=30$ evaluations $+ 3$ warmups per block with continuous 50 ms Apple SMC power telemetry, saved in `results/publication_campaign/tool_rag_mitigation_trials.jsonl`, `tool_rag_mitigation_summary.json`, and `tables/table_tool_rag_mitigation.tex`) demonstrates that semantic pruning adds merely $0.033\text{--}0.038\text{ ms}$ per turn in Go ($0.382\text{ ms}$ across 10 turns in W4) and $0.286\text{--}0.427\text{ ms}$ per turn in Node.js V8 ($3.330\text{--}4.268\text{ ms}$ in W4) while shrinking serialized prompt tokens from $7,067\text{--}7,499$ to $893$ in W1 ($72,120\text{--}76,440$ to $9,197\text{--}10,380$ across 10 turns in W4).
  - Consequently, the active Tool-RAG Pruner eliminates **$85.5\%\text{ to }87.2\%$ of end-to-end latency and $85.6\%\text{ to }87.8\%$ of electrical energy** relative to the unpruned 54-tool catalog in Regime A ($50.9\%\text{ to }54.6\%$ faster than even the static 15-tool catalog), and in Regime B reduces latency by $21.1\%\text{ to }37.9\%$ vs. 54 tools, outperforming even the static 15-tool catalog ($952.4\text{ ms}$ vs. $1033.5\text{ ms}$ in W1; $8334.2\text{--}8358.8\text{ ms}$ vs. $8605.5\text{ ms}$ in W4).

## 2. Refutation of Darwin XNU `ri_billed_energy` as a Validated Negative Result & Validated Physical CPU Regressors ($R^2 = 0.955$) (Resolves M1, X1, C1, M8)
- **Reviewer Concern:** Using zero-intercept OLS calibration ($\hat{\kappa}_{\text{M4}} \cdot \texttt{ri\_billed\_energy}$) produced a low global $R^2 = 0.0538$ and inflated CPython's estimated Regime B orchestrator energy share to $25.87\% / 71.13\%$.
- **Resolution (`scripts/run_uma_energy_and_bandwidth_attribution.py`, `uma_energy_bandwidth_attribution.json`, `table_energy_bandwidth_deconvolution.tex`, `tests/test_artifact_consistency.py`):**
  - We **completely removed** $\hat{\kappa}_{\text{M4}} \cdot \texttt{ri\_billed\_energy}$ as a Joule estimator and **formally retracted** the $25.87\% / 71.13\%$ figures.
  - We reframed Darwin XNU's `ri_billed_energy` counter as a **Validated Negative Result**: across all 120 isolated CPU phase trials (`uma_energy_bandwidth_trials.jsonl`), `ri_billed_energy` exhibits zero global linear correlation with physical Apple SMC `CPU Power` rail Joules ($r = -0.0510, p = 0.580$; intra-agent $r = -0.10$ for Go, $-0.08$ for Python, $+0.07$ for Node.js Minimal) and a **negative centered coefficient of determination ($\text{OLS } R^2 = -0.676$ relative to the sample mean)**. This proves mathematically that uncalibrated XNU billing units perform worse than a constant mean predictor and overestimate CPython energy by $10\text{--}30\times$ due to internal XNU scheduler QoS/thread-weighting heuristics, directly corroborating **Fahad et al. (2019)** (*Energies* 12(11), 2204, reporting 73% average and up to 300% maximum error in on-chip energy estimation models).
  - We established and saved three **Validated Physical CPU Energy Regressors** against net SMC `CPU Power` rail Joules ($E_{\text{CPU, net}}$):
    1. Execution time (`t_exec_ms`): **Pearson $r = 0.992$, centered $\text{OLS } R^2 = 0.949$** (intra-agent $\text{corr}(t, E_{\text{CPU, net}}) = 1.00$ for Go, $0.99$ for Node-Minimal, $0.85$ for Python);
    2. Retired CPU instructions (`proc_instructions`): **Pearson $r = 0.985$, centered $\text{OLS } R^2 = 0.955$**;
    3. Core CPU cycles (`proc_cycles`): **Pearson $r = 0.991$, centered $\text{OLS } R^2 = 0.945$**.
  - We added 95% Bootstrap CIs (2,000 resamples) to all 8 isolated `mJ/op` measurements and computed the Regime B orchestrator energy fraction ($\Phi_{\text{energy, orch}} = E_{\text{CPU, net}}^{\text{dispatch}} / E_{\text{net, task}}$) **exclusively from direct physical SMC `CPU Power` rail energy**:
    - **PicoClaw (Go Native):** `0.04% / 1.42%` ($3.76\text{ mJ} / 8.86\text{ J}$ in W2; $136.62\text{ mJ} / 9.64\text{ J}$ in W3)
    - **PythonNative (CPython):** `0.03% / 0.34%` ($3.00\text{ mJ} / 9.70\text{ J}$ in W2; $34.08\text{ mJ} / 9.92\text{ J}$ in W3)
    - **OpenClaw Minimal (V8):** `0.01% / 0.49%` ($0.59\text{ mJ} / 9.95\text{ J}$ in W2; $45.67\text{ mJ} / 9.38\text{ J}$ in W3)
    - **OpenClaw Full (V8):** `0.01% / 0.30%` ($0.53\text{ mJ} / 11.00\text{ J}$ in W2; $44.36\text{ mJ} / 14.74\text{ J}$ in W3)
  - We added unit test `test_energy_regressor_validity_and_spurious_rejection` in `tests/test_artifact_consistency.py` enforcing $R^2 \ge 0.90$ for valid physical proxies and verifying `rejected_spurious_proxy = True` ($|r| < 0.15$) for `ri_billed_energy`.

Every single one of the **660 records** in `warm_cache_trials.jsonl` now has `ttft_ms > 0` and `itl_ms > 0` populated (`0` missing values), and all contrasts within W1, W2, W3, and W4 are 100% intra-session.
  - **Native Bun Installation & Rigorous $n=30$ Inter-Session Replication:** We installed native **Bun v1.4.4 (`darwin-aarch64`, JavaScriptCore)** at `tools/bun/bin/bun` and updated `BunMinimalAdapter` so `is_fallback_node = False` and `runner_binary_used = "bun"`. We rewrote `scripts/run_inter_session_replication.py` with **zero `try...except` fallbacks**, $n=30$ evaluations $+ 3$ primings per randomized block (`rng = random.Random(99); rng.shuffle(blocks)`), a single continuous `SMCPowerMonitor` daemon, child-process `run_subprocess_with_rusage` memory telemetry (zero `os.getpid()`), full raw trial logging (`results/publication_campaign/inter_session_trials.jsonl`), and exact metrics `inter_session_rel_diff_pct` ($|m_1 - m_2|/\bar{m}$, ranging from $0.22\%$ to $3.32\%$, mean $1.62\%$) and `inter_session_pooled_cv_pct` ($\text{SD}(\{m_1, m_2\})/\bar{m}$, mean $1.15\%$), plus paired intra-session contrasts against each session's `neural_baseline`.
